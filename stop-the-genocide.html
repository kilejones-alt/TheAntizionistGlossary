STOP THE GENOCIDE

“Stop the Genocide” is a protest slogan that treats Israel’s war in Gaza as an ongoing genocide and demands urgent action against Israel and everyone who supports it. It announces a legal conclusion that no court has reached.

Raphael Lemkin coined the word genocide in Axis Rule in Occupied Europe in 1944, and four years later it entered treaty law through the Genocide Convention. The word was born from the destruction of European Jewry. Article II of the Convention defines genocide as certain acts committed with intent to destroy, in whole or in part, a national, ethnical, racial, or religious group as such. Mass death, displacement, hunger, and even grave breaches of the laws of war do not, by themselves, satisfy that definition. A commander who bombs a city recklessly may be guilty of a war crime, and a state that fights brutally may answer for crimes against humanity. Genocide requires the purpose of erasing a people as a people. The libel is that Israel is trying to destroy Palestinians as Palestinians.

After the Hamas-led attacks of October 7, 2023, the chant spread through marches and divestment drives, campus encampments and ceasefire resolutions, and the letters of clergy and professional associations. It carried demands for arms embargoes, sanctions, and the boycott of Israeli universities and artists. Amnesty International concluded in December 2024 that Israel had committed and was still committing genocide against Palestinians in Gaza. Médecins Sans Frontières adopted genocide language in its Gaza materials. In September 2025, the UN Independent International Commission of Inquiry on the Occupied Palestinian Territory reached the same conclusion. None of these bodies is a court, none conducted a trial, and none tested its conclusion against an adversary entitled to reply.

The case brought by South Africa at the International Court of Justice remains pending. The Court’s order of January 26, 2024 indicated provisional measures and made no finding of genocide. Former ICJ President Joan Donoghue later explained to the BBC that the Court had found a plausible right of Palestinians to protection from genocide and had allowed South Africa to bring its case. South Africa filed its memorial in October 2024, Israel filed its response in March 2026, and as of June 2, 2026 the Court had issued no judgment on the merits. The slogan takes the judgment as already entered.

The International Criminal Court moved on a separate track. In November 2024, it issued arrest warrants for Benjamin Netanyahu and Yoav Gallant for alleged war crimes and crimes against humanity, among them starvation as a method of warfare, murder, persecution, and other inhumane acts. Genocide was not among them.

The slogan carries three claims, that genocide is occurring, that Israel is its author, and that anyone who supports Israel is an accomplice. Once genocide is the premise, ordinary contact with Israel is complicity, and the guilt reaches the donor and the synagogue. Everything Hamas did on and after October 7, the massacre and the hostages, the tunnels beneath civilian buildings, the fire drawn from inside homes and hospitals, is set aside as a distraction. Israel’s warnings, evacuation corridors, entry of aid, and the legal duties of a state fighting an armed group in a dense city are dismissed as an excuse. A lawful-war analysis asks what was targeted, what was known at the moment of the strike, what precautions were feasible, and whether unlawful intent can be proved.

The phrase changes how Jews are heard. A Jew who asks for evidence is called a defender of genocide. A Jewish institution that affirms Israel’s right to exist is treated as an accessory to it. People who survived the Holocaust are named as the authors of the very crime that gave the postwar legal order its name.

The word was aimed at Israel long before 2023. During the Lebanon War of 1982, UN General Assembly Resolution 37/123 declared the massacre at Sabra and Shatila “an act of genocide.” The killing was carried out by the Lebanese Christian Phalangist militia. Israel’s own Kahan Commission found the militia directly responsible and held Israeli officials indirectly responsible for failing to foresee and prevent the danger. Soviet and allied rhetoric had painted Zionism as racist, colonial, and exterminatory. UN General Assembly Resolution 3379, passed in 1975, declared Zionism a form of racism, and the Durban NGO Forum of 2001 carried that habit into the language of human rights. After October 7, court filings and NGO reports, campus campaigns and clergy statements, the petitions of artists and the graphics of social media made genocide the default word for Israel’s war in Gaza.

At demonstrations the slogan is an emergency command. If genocide is happening at this very moment, delay sounds like complicity and a demand for evidence sounds like evasion. The urgency drives boycotts, severed partnerships, arms restrictions, and public denunciation. A speaker who raises the hostages, or the fighters embedded in civilian areas, or the legal gulf between a war crime and a genocide, is branded an apologist. The phrase also reverses the burden of proof. Israel and its defenders are made to prove that the war is not genocidal, while in law a crime defined by special intent must be proved by whoever asserts it. The destruction in Gaza has been immense and the death toll heavy. Suffering is the common consequence of war, and genocide is a separate finding about purpose.
