BUTCHER(S) OF GAZA

“Butcher(s) of Gaza” is a phrase aimed at Israeli leaders and military officials, and sometimes at American officials tied to support for Israel. Its purpose is to present civilian deaths in Gaza as deliberate slaughter. The label asks none of the questions the law of war asks, whether a strike was lawful, whether the target was military, whether precautions were taken, or whether Hamas fought from civilian sites. “Butcher of Gaza” is no legal category and requires no case file, tribunal, rule of evidence, or proof of intent. It converts a disputed battlefield into a portrait of bloodlust.

The word travels. It starts with Benjamin Netanyahu and slides outward to Israeli soldiers, to American presidents, to university trustees and donors, to Jewish organizations and Jewish students accused of defending or normalizing Israel. Along that path the word stops naming a particular person and assigns blood guilt by association. A word built for a head of state ends up pinned to a Jewish freshman at a campus rally.

The phrase gained wide international reach in late November 2023, when Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan said Netanyahu would be remembered as the “butcher of Gaza.” On December 4, 2023, Reuters reported Erdogan calling Netanyahu “the butcher of Gaza right now” and vowing he would be tried as such, with a comparison to Slobodan Milosevic. A head of state had given the phrase diplomatic weight, and protest movements picked up the vocabulary within days. In demonstrations and online activism it rides alongside images of blood and dead children, war-crimes language and demands for prosecution. It is often paired with “child killer,” “genocide,” or “war criminal.” “War criminal” at least gestures toward a legal standard that could be met or refuted. “Butcher” invites the listener to picture cruelty as the motive and to skip the part where motive has to be shown.

The label has also entered campus materials. In 2025, The New York War Crimes, a protest publication tied to Writers Against the War on Gaza, ran a piece titled “Dismantle the Board of Butchers!” that bound university trustees to imperialism, Zionism and genocide. That October, the American Jewish Committee, the American Council on Education, Hillel International, and the Association of Governing Boards warned that the campaign was branding trustees as “butchers” with calls to “break the butchers” and “dismantle the board of butchers.” The word had reached the governing boards of American universities. The ADL’s 2024 report on anti-Israel activism on U.S. campuses counted 2,637 incidents of assault and vandalism, harassment, protests and divestment resolutions between June 1, 2023 and May 31, 2024.

The word presumes that civilian deaths in Gaza flow from an Israeli intent to kill civilians. Civilian death by itself does not prove unlawful targeting, and an unlawful strike in one instance does not prove a general policy of massacre. Hamas operates from or beside civilian sites, among them schools and mosques, hospitals and apartment blocks, and the tunnel network dug beneath crowded neighborhoods. The NATO Strategic Communications Centre of Excellence, West Point’s Lieber Institute, and Israeli government sources have all described this pattern. The butcher word needs the battlefield emptied of everyone but the accused. It carries a theory of the war, that Israel kills because killing Palestinians is the aim. Once a listener accepts that premise, every civilian death is fresh confirmation.

The word is old and common in antiwar speech, and it has been hurled at generals and heads of state of every description. Aimed at Jews it strikes an older chord, because hostile movements have for centuries tied Jews to blood, slaughter and ritual murder. In 1900 in Konitz, Prussia, now Chojnice, Poland, an eighteen-year-old named Ernst Winter was found murdered and dismembered, and antisemitic agitators and newspapers steered suspicion onto the local Jewish community. The blame settled on Adolph Lewy, a Jewish butcher, and the riots that followed were severe enough that the Prussian army had to step in. No Jew was ever convicted. Helmut Walser Smith’s The Butcher’s Tale shows how an ordinary trade served, in antisemitic hands, as a vehicle for the fantasy of Jewish bloodlust.

The modern Israel-related version surfaced most prominently after the 1982 Sabra and Shatila massacre. The killings were carried out by Lebanese Christian Phalangist forces while Israeli troops controlled access to the camps. Israel’s Kahan Commission found that the Phalangists bore direct responsibility and that Israel bore indirect responsibility, and it held Defense Minister Ariel Sharon personally responsible for failing to foresee and prevent the danger, recommending he leave the Defense Ministry. Sharon was never found to have ordered or carried out the massacre. The labels “Butcher of Beirut” and “Butcher of Sabra and Shatila” reduced that layered finding to one red image. After October 7 the word was taken up again almost at once.

A legal claim can be tested, and a blood image is far harder to answer. If a speaker has already decided an Israeli leader is a butcher, any rebuttal sounds like a defense of murder. The term also doubles as a loyalty test. A student who refuses to say it can be cast as indifferent to Palestinian suffering, and a Jewish student who notes that Hamas bears responsibility too, or that intent must be proved, can be painted as a protector of butchers. The campus use against trustees widens the circle of the guilty. If an institution invests in or works with firms linked to Israel, its board members and donors, administrators and students are butchers too.

The same pressure reaches international institutions, where ICC warrants, war-crimes arguments and state referrals all require evidence of specific crimes. The butcher chant produces an audience taught to resent the demand for proof. War-crimes law runs on rules of distinction and proportionality, precautions and command responsibility, intent and knowledge, and the slur swaps every one of those rules for a picture. Its slide from Netanyahu to soldiers to Zionists to Jewish students is the danger itself, the moment a slogan about a war turns into a suspicion about a people.
