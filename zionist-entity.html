ZIONIST ENTITY

“Zionist Entity” is a phrase of state denial. It refers to Israel without once naming it and withholds recognition from the state itself. The word “entity” makes Israel sound artificial, temporary, and illegitimate, a foreign implant awaiting removal, in place of a state with citizens, borders, institutions, and international recognition.

It appears in Arab nationalist, Islamist, Iranian, and antizionist rhetoric, in official speeches, propaganda outlets, militant statements, protest material, and online discourse. It commonly appears alongside “occupation,” “settler-colonialism,” “liberation,” and “resistance.” In English-language activism it is less common than “apartheid state” or “settler-colonial state,” and it does the same work. The state is treated as a thing whose existence is improper.

The speaker refuses the ordinary name of the country because the name concedes too much. To say “Israel” is to acknowledge a state.

The expression belongs to the vocabulary of Arab rejectionism after Israel’s founding. It was used by states and movements that rejected Jewish sovereignty and refused to normalize Israel’s name or legitimacy. Iranian and Islamist rhetoric describes Israel in the same way, as an occupying, Zionist, or usurping entity.

The choice of the word entity repays attention. It is the word for a thing that happens to be standing somewhere, and it carries no claim on anyone’s recognition. Borders are negotiated with a state. An entity is eliminated, and the man who says Zionist entity has found a way to mean destroy Israel without the awkwardness of pronouncing it. Millions of Jewish and Arab citizens live inside the country the word declines to name, and the phrase has no use for them except as an abstraction. Once they are one, boycott, violence, diplomatic rejection, and endless war all become easy to justify.
