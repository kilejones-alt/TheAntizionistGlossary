JEWISH SUPREMACY

“Jewish supremacy” is a label used in anti-Israel and antizionist writing to cast Israel as a system built to privilege Jews over Palestinians. B’Tselem used it in 2021 in the title of its paper, “A regime of Jewish supremacy from the Jordan River to the Mediterranean Sea: This is apartheid,” and Adalah, the Legal Center for Arab Minority Rights in Israel, uses related terms in its legal and advocacy work. The formulation draws its force from its resemblance to white supremacy.

Narrowly, the label makes a legal claim about Israeli rule in the West Bank, about settlements, East Jerusalem and Gaza, about the Nation-State Law, land policy and the unequal treatment of Palestinians. In antizionist hands it treats Jewish national self-determination as domination in itself. The target is the Jewish state because it is Jewish.

B’Tselem’s 2021 position paper argued that the whole area between the Jordan River and the Mediterranean is organized around one principle, the advancement of Jewish supremacy over Palestinians, and Adalah’s materials on the Nation-State Law say the statute enshrines Jewish supremacy over Palestinian citizens of Israel. Yesh Din adopted the same apartheid and supremacy language in the years around 2020. The phrase spread after October 7 through campus protest and BDS material, social media, teach-ins and faculty letters, traveling beside apartheid, settler-colonialism, ethnostate, and Zionist racism. In that company it is shorthand for Zionism itself. White supremacy is among the clearest civic evils in American life, and transferring its grammar to Zionism asks the listener whether to oppose supremacy.

Jewish collective rights are held to differ in kind from the collective rights of other peoples. National symbols and a national language, a Jewish immigration preference, Hebrew calendar references, Jewish holidays, and concern for the diaspora are offered as evidence of racial hierarchy. Israel’s 2018 Nation-State Basic Law is the strongest source critics have. It declares Israel the nation-state of the Jewish people, says the right to national self-determination in Israel is exclusive to the Jewish people, names Hebrew the state language while giving Arabic special status, holds the state open to Jewish immigration, and calls Jewish settlement a national value. Israel also has Basic Law: Human Dignity and Liberty, which states that its purpose is to anchor the values of Israel as a Jewish and democratic state. Basic Law: The Knesset provides that every Israeli citizen of eighteen or older may vote unless a court has removed the right under law.

Arab citizens make up roughly a fifth of the population. They vote and run parties, sit in the Knesset and serve in government coalitions, argue before the courts and sit on them. On June 2, 2021, Ra’am, an Islamist party led by Mansour Abbas, was the first independent Arab faction to sit in a coalition, trading its votes in the Knesset for billions of shekels aimed at Arab towns. On May 9, 2022, the Supreme Court seated Khaled Kabub, who had spent decades on the Tel Aviv District bench, as its first permanent Muslim member, through the regular appointments committee. A system built on permanent caste does not hand the people it keeps at the bottom a ballot, a robe, and a chair at the cabinet table.

The phrase descends from a Cold War propaganda effort that predates every settlement and checkpoint now invoked to justify it. Soviet and Arab-state campaigns after 1967 treated Zionism as racial domination and pushed that wording into UN forums. The UN General Assembly branded Zionism a form of racial discrimination through Resolution 3379 on November 10, 1975, and Israel’s ambassador Chaim Herzog tore the text apart at the podium. The same body struck the language down through Resolution 46/86 on December 16, 1991, the price of bringing Israel to the Madrid peace talks. The wording returned, almost unchanged, in the chants heard at today’s protests.

It works as a disqualification device. It tells the audience that Zionism need not be debated, because it has been filed under racial domination, and ordinary contact with Israel is redescribed as complicity with supremacy. The burden then shifts onto Jews. A Jewish student or scholar, an artist or donor, a synagogue or Hillel chapter or Israeli speaker can be asked to prove distance from Zionism before being treated as acceptable. The question put to them is “will you reject Jewish supremacy?”

The phrase slides between a softer and a harder meaning. Challenged, the speaker says it refers only to unequal Israeli policy. In movement use it means that a Jewish state is illegitimate as such. Once a Jewish state is assumed to be wrong for being Jewish, the Law of Return, Hebrew on official forms, and the symbols on the flag look like proof. No other nation’s self-rule draws the same label, whether Greek, Japanese or Irish.
