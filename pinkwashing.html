PINKWASHING

“Pinkwashing” is the antizionist claim that Israel uses LGBT rights to distract from its conduct toward Palestinians. The word did not begin with Israel. Breast Cancer Action says it coined the term through its Think Before You Pink campaign to describe companies or organizations that wrap themselves in breast-cancer concern while profiting from products linked to cancer risk. Anti-Israel activists later adapted the word to Israel, and a civil-rights difference was made evidence of deceit.

Israel decriminalized consensual same-sex conduct in 1988. Sexual-orientation employment protection followed in 1992. In El-Al Israel Airlines v. Danilowitz, the Israeli Supreme Court recognized same-sex partner benefits in 1994. Israel later recognized some same-sex family rights, registered same-sex marriages performed abroad, and opened surrogacy access to same-sex couples and single men after a 2021 Supreme Court ruling. The claim admits those facts and treats mentioning them as suspect. If Israel mistreated gay people, antizionists would cite the mistreatment as proof of cruelty. When Israel protects gay people, the protection is described as public relations. Israel cannot answer by improving the lives of LGBT Israelis, because improvement itself is evidence against it.

The Israel-specific use spread after Sarah Schulman’s 2011 New York Times essay, “Israel and ‘Pinkwashing.’” Schulman argued that Israeli officials promoted gay rights to distract from Palestinian suffering and from Israel’s rule over Palestinians. Her later book, Israel/Palestine and the Queer International, placed the claim inside a wider activist project. A second route came through Jasbir Puar’s work on homonationalism, which argued that some states incorporate certain gay subjects into national self-presentation while casting Muslim or racialized others as backward or dangerous. Antizionists used that theory to describe Israeli gay rights as state power in disguise.

Israel has promoted Tel Aviv, tourism, and LGBT rights abroad. It also has religious marriage rules and social hostility toward gay people in some communities. Pinkwashing treats every Israeli LGBT advance as propaganda, every comparison with neighboring societies as bigotry, and every Jewish or Israeli queer institution as politically contaminated unless it first denounces the Jewish state.

The term makes Palestinian LGBT vulnerability difficult to discuss. Palestinian LGBT people have faced danger from families, armed groups, police, social hostility, and rulers who do not protect them. In 2019, Palestinian Authority police announced a ban on the activities of alQaws, a Palestinian LGBT organization, after threats against the group, and after backlash the ban was reportedly withdrawn. Pinkwashing language turns such facts into forbidden evidence because the comparison weakens the preferred conclusion.

After October 7, the pressure moved from rhetoric to institutional exclusion. ILGA World suspended The Aguda, Israel’s main LGBT rights organization, in October 2024, then announced in May 2025 that the suspension would be lifted effective October 27, 2025 after review. In May 2026, Reuters reported that Rome Pride had barred Keshet Italia, Italy’s only Jewish LGBT group, from joining the parade with a float because Keshet had not explicitly condemned Israel’s actions in Gaza as genocide. Rome Pride said the event remained open to all who shared its values. Keshet Italia said the decision imposed a litmus test on a Jewish LGBT group.

Pinkwashing begins as criticism of Israeli state messaging and ends as a rule for disciplining queer Jewish and Israeli participation in public life. Pride organizers, universities, arts spaces, unions, and NGOs are pressured to exclude Israelis or Jews unless they recite the required politics. A movement built around LGBT belonging tells LGBT Jews that they belong only if they detach themselves from Israel. The term makes Israel guilty whether it fails gay people or protects them.
