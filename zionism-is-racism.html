Zionism Is Racism

“Zionism is racism” is a slogan that defines the Jewish national movement as racial supremacy. It names no border, coalition, settlement, court decision, or military tactic. Zionism, the movement for Jewish self-determination in the ancestral land of Israel, is cast as the equivalent of racial domination, and the formula condemns the premise that Jews may exist as a sovereign people.

The phrase appears on protest signs, in faculty letters, in student resolutions, in boycott campaigns, and in activist accounts of Israel as an apartheid or settler-colonial state. It also appears in arguments that hold Zionism interchangeable with white supremacy, fascism, Nazism, or colonial violence.

The line belongs to the Cold War history of Soviet, Arab-bloc, and Third World diplomacy against Israel. After Israel’s victory in 1967, the Soviet Union intensified its drive to portray Zionism as racist, imperialist, colonial, and fascist, and Arab states and their allies pushed the same language through international forums. In 1973, General Assembly Resolution 3151G linked Zionism with South African racism and Portuguese colonialism. On November 10, 1975, the General Assembly adopted Resolution 3379, which declared that “Zionism is a form of racism and racial discrimination.” The General Assembly revoked it in 1991 through Resolution 46/86. The wording remained available to campus movements, Soviet-influenced literature, Arab nationalist rhetoric and BDS campaigns. The NGO Forum at the Durban Conference in 2001 described Israel as a racist, apartheid state and called for measures modeled on the campaign against South Africa. BDS, launched in 2005, drew heavily from that atmosphere.

Zionism is a national movement and holds no racial theory. Jews are not a race, and Israeli Jews come from Europe, the Middle East, North Africa, Ethiopia, the former Soviet Union, the Americas, and elsewhere. Zionism argues that Jews are a people entitled to self-determination in their ancestral homeland. Many states have national symbols, immigration laws, official languages, religious heritage, and majority cultures, and Israel alone is treated as contaminated by them.

Those three words treat Jewish statehood as illegitimate by nature. Other national movements may be called flawed, violent, or historically burdened. A state that is racist by nature cannot be repaired by better policy and must be dismantled. Zionism arose because Jews were repeatedly unsafe as minorities under other peoples’ rule, and the chant turns that response into the crime. Jewish refuge, Jewish power, and Jewish self-defense appear as threats in place of answers to centuries of exclusion, pogroms, expulsion, and genocide.

The slogan puts its conclusion before the evidence. Arab citizenship in Israel, Arab parties and Arab judges, peace offers, wars launched against Israel, suicide bombings, rockets, hostages, and Hamas’s stated aims are all secondary. It moves quickly to the territories, because citizenship inside Israel weakens the racial-state claim.

A person who accepts it is inside the antizionist moral community, and a person who rejects it is taken to be defending racism. If Zionism is racism, Zionists are racists, Israeli Jews are beneficiaries of racism, and Jewish institutions that support Israel are suspect. For Jewish students and institutions, support for Israel’s existence is evidence of contamination. The slogan leaves no room for two states or compromise and pushes the debate toward the end of Jewish sovereignty.
